Understanding your coverage

Most people choose a plan by looking at the monthly premium and stopping there. That's a reasonable first filter, but it misses the number that actually determines whether a plan works for you: your out-of-pocket maximum — the most you'll pay in a bad year before the plan covers the rest.

Premium vs. deductible vs. out-of-pocket maximum

The premium is what you pay every month regardless of whether you use care. The deductible is what you pay before coverage kicks in for most services. The out-of-pocket maximum is your ceiling — once you hit it, the plan covers 100% for the rest of the year. A low-premium plan often has a high out-of-pocket maximum, which is fine if you're healthy and risky if you're not.

Rule of thumb: if you have dependents or a known condition, weigh the out-of-pocket maximum more heavily than the premium.

What our model actually optimizes for

Our matching model looks at your age, dependents, existing conditions, and typical care usage, then estimates your likely annual cost under each plan — premium plus expected out-of-pocket spend — rather than premium alone. That's why the "cheapest" plan on paper isn't always the one we recommend first.
